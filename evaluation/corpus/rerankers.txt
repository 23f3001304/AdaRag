Cross-encoder reranking

A first-stage retriever has to be fast, so it compares precomputed vectors and never reads the query and a passage together. A reranker pays for precision instead. It feeds the query and a candidate passage into a single transformer at once, letting every query token attend to every passage token, and emits one relevance score for the pair.

This joint attention is what makes the second stage sharper than vector similarity, because the model can tell that a passage mentions the query's subject only in passing rather than truly answering it. AdaRag runs a bge-reranker-v2-m3 cross-encoder over the fused candidate pool and keeps only the reordered top results for the generator.

The cost is latency: scoring twenty candidates means twenty transformer passes, so the reranker runs on a short list rather than the whole collection. Choosing how many candidates to admit is a tuning decision that trades recall against speed, and it is one of the knobs the pipeline sweeps.
